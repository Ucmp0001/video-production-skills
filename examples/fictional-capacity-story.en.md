# Fictional exercise: orders doubled, so why is delivery backed up?

[中文](fictional-capacity-story.md) · [Beginner guide](../docs/getting-started.en.md)

These are invented assumptions for learning, not a real company, news event or person. Do not publish this as a factual business investigation.

## Inputs and limits
F1: An illustrative factory completes 10 equal-workload orders per day; capacity is temporarily fixed.
F2: Previous demand was 8 orders per day; new demand is 16.
F3: No added capacity, overtime, cancellations or productivity changes.
F4: Initial backlog is 0. Each day orders arrive before production; unfinished orders carry into the next day.
F5: Backlog is 6 after day 1, 12 after day 2 and 18 after day 3. This model ignores real-world variability, order sizes and inventory.

## Creative task
Explain demand growth and delivery backlog to a general audience in an estimated 60 seconds. Open with “Orders doubled—why is delivery now stuck?” Derive values only from the assumptions above; add no real people, deals, media sources or implied news scene.

## Available and missing resources
You can create 10 capacity slots and daily order queues, optionally combining 2D labels with 3D factory/box objects. No real factory footage, recording, music, Logo, voice service or measured timestamps are supplied. Script duration is an estimate; final shot timing requires actual audio.

## What to verify
Capacity remains 10. Backlogs are 6, 12 and 18. Show arriving orders, production and carried-over orders before revealing result labels; the factory must not automatically expand with demand. Close by answering: “Capacity stayed the same, so 6 extra orders remain every day.” Preserve the fictional-assumption label.
